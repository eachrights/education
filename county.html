Kilifi's Financial Position

KSh billion

Pending bills are money the county owes suppliers and others. A large stock of them can slow how fully education allocations are paid out.

Fiscal pressure. Pending bills are equal to about of a year's revenue. This compares a September 2026 balance with 2024/25 revenue, so read it as a rough scale rather than an exact ratio.
Cash flow in 2025/26. By 31 March 2026 Kilifi had received KSh 8.52 billion of its KSh 12.81 billion equitable share (66.5%), below the 75% pro-rata benchmark; the Controller of Budget gives 66% and notes that most counties received a similar share [8]. Kilifi's development absorption for the year was 84.5% across all departments, one of only four counties above 75%. This is not specific to education [9].
